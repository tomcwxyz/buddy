# Buddy

**A child-centred learning companion for reading, learning and discovering how you think.**

Buddy is not intended to be a dyslexia test, a reading score, or a generic AI tutor. It is a persistent, supportive companion that helps a child when something is difficult, learns which kinds of support work for them, creates small opportunities for useful practice, and gradually helps them understand how they learn best.

The initial focus is children aged roughly 9–12, beginning with reading support for dyslexia. The longer-term ambition is broader: learning agency, self-understanding and strengths.

## Core idea

Buddy follows four connected experiences:

- **Read** — help me with what I am doing now.
- **Practice** — remember useful patterns and help me revisit them playfully.
- **Discover** — help me find out how I learn and what I am good at.
- **Do** — help me tackle schoolwork and other tasks without doing the thinking for me.

The companion is the product. A phone, tablet, web app, Rabbit R1, Android device or future dedicated object is simply a surface for the same Buddy, with the same learning memory and child-controlled support.

## Design principles

1. **Capability, not deficit.** Difficulty is normal. Asking for help is normal. Buddy should build agency, not repeatedly label what a child cannot do.
2. **Preserve the task.** If the child is reading a story, enjoying and understanding the story matters more than turning every difficult word into a lesson.
3. **Scaffold rather than answer.** Buddy gives the smallest useful amount of help and allows the child to ask for more.
4. **Child control.** The child controls how much help Buddy gives, what it remembers, and when it should stop.
5. **Multi-modal by default.** Voice, vision, touch, listening, images, physical controls and reading are all valid ways to interact.
6. **Visible memory.** Buddy should be able to explain what it remembers and let the child correct or delete it.
7. **Privacy by architecture.** No advertising, engagement optimisation or hidden child profiling.
8. **Strengths are discovered, not assigned.** Buddy should never tell a child they have a standard set of “dyslexic superpowers”.

## Surfaces

Buddy is designed as a multi-surface system from the start:

- phone;
- tablet;
- web/laptop;
- Rabbit R1 and similar tactile devices;
- commodity Android hardware;
- Bluetooth headphones;
- future dedicated Buddy hardware;
- carefully scoped external agents.

Different surfaces should not reproduce the same UI. A tablet may be visual and exploratory; a small tactile device may use only a camera, voice, a button, a dial and a very small display.

## First product milestone

The first useful Buddy should be deliberately small:

1. point a phone or tablet camera at a page;
2. select or point to a difficult word;
3. hear it;
4. break it down appropriately;
5. ask what it means in context;
6. ask a spoken follow-up;
7. save it to **My Words**;
8. later offer a very short, personalised practice activity.

The major early product question is not technical: **does a child actually want Buddy beside them while reading?**
